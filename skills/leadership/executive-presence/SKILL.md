# Executive Presence

## Operating standard

Treat executive presence as observable behavior in context, not personality conformity, accent, charisma, appearance, or dominance. Preserve authentic style while improving decision usefulness and trust.

## Inputs

Obtain the setting, audience, decision, stakes, time available, power dynamics, likely objections, communication sample or observed behavior, desired reputation, cultural context, and prior feedback.

## Workflow

1. Define what the audience must understand, believe, decide, or do.
2. Assess the evidence across clarity, judgment, composure, credibility, listening, brevity, and ownership.
3. Identify the strongest behavior and the one or two behaviors creating the largest confidence penalty.
4. Separate content weakness from delivery weakness and situational bias.
5. Structure the message as conclusion, rationale, evidence, trade-off, recommendation, and ask.
6. Prepare concise responses to challenge, uncertainty, disagreement, and missing data.
7. Design observable practice drills and a feedback method.
8. Review outcomes after the event and update the behavior target.

## Decision rules

- Prefer a clear decision and trade-off over a dense display of expertise.
- State uncertainty without surrendering ownership of the recommendation.
- Slow down when stakes or emotion rise; shorten when time or attention falls.
- Answer the question before adding context.
- Do not coach imitation of another leader's style when the needed behavior can be expressed authentically.

## Output

Provide:

1. Situational objective and audience stakes
2. Evidence-based presence diagnosis
3. One primary and one supporting behavior target
4. Message architecture and opening
5. Challenge-response preparation
6. Practice drill and success measure
7. Post-event reflection questions

## Quality check

- Feedback refers to observable behavior and impact.
- Recommendations do not reward dominance, jargon, or performative certainty.
- The message has a clear decision, rationale, trade-off, and ask.
- Practice is specific enough to repeat and measure.
- Cultural and power context are considered without stereotyping.

## Failure recovery

If no sample or observation exists, state that the diagnosis is provisional and begin with a simulated scenario. If feedback conflicts, identify the audience and context behind each signal before generalizing.

## Example

**Request:** “Help me sound more executive when the CFO challenges my technology investment.”

Create a concise decision narrative, challenge responses, and a practice drill grounded in judgment and financial trade-offs.

## Evaluation

A strong result improves confidence through clarity, judgment, composure, and authenticity—not theatrical polish.
