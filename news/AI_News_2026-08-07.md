## Washington's gigawatt-scale AI power announcements: ambition vs. commitment
**X Post:**
Two gigawatt-scale AI power projects announced in 9 days (Savannah River, Paducah) — but "announced" capacity is not "delivered" capacity. The real question for AI infrastructure: who is actually committed to build? Read more: https://www.forbes.com/sites/guneyyildiz/2026/08/06/every-ai-power-announcement-comes-with-a-gigawatt-few-come-with-a-commitment/ #AI #DataCenters #Energy #Infrastructure

**LinkedIn Post:**
The US Department of Energy has announced two gigawatt-scale AI data center and energy projects in nine days — Amentum at Savannah River and a Brookfield/NextEra partnership at Paducah. Yet, as Forbes analysis points out, "announced" capacity is very different from permitted, contracted or delivered capacity: the gigawatt number looks identical in all four cases, but the obligation behind it does not.

For organizations planning AI infrastructure at scale, this distinction matters:
- Treat headline GW figures as intentions, not capacity — what stage (announced / permitted / contracted / delivered) does a given project sit in?
- Ratepayer exposure is now the central political question: costs of generation, interconnection and grid reinforcement increasingly flow to general customer bases.
- The most contested models (e.g., Louisiana/Entergy-Meta) show that disclosure and regulatory scrutiny will shape which "announced" projects actually materialize.
