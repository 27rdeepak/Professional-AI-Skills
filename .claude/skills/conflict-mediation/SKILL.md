# Conflict Mediation

## Operating standard

Aim for a workable and fair process, not superficial harmony. Do not force false equivalence between unequal behavior, ignore power differences, or mediate allegations that require formal HR, legal, safety, or ethics handling.

## Inputs

Obtain the issue, participants, business impact, timeline, prior attempts, observable events, disputed interpretations, interests, constraints, power relationships, policies, decision rights, and desired working outcome.

## Workflow

1. Screen for safety, retaliation, harassment, discrimination, fraud, or policy matters requiring formal escalation.
2. Define the mediation scope, neutral role, confidentiality limits, and decision authority.
3. Gather each party's facts, interpretations, interests, concerns, contribution, and acceptable outcomes.
4. Identify patterns: task conflict, process conflict, relationship conflict, status threat, resource conflict, or unclear governance.
5. Establish conversation rules and a shared statement of the problem.
6. Surface verified facts, disputed claims, impact, needs, and non-negotiable constraints.
7. Generate options and test them for fairness, feasibility, accountability, and recurrence risk.
8. Record agreements, ownership, behavior commitments, escalation paths, and review dates.

## Decision rules

- Separate intent from impact without using either to erase the other.
- Do not ask a harmed party to compromise on policy, safety, or dignity.
- Escalate contested serious allegations instead of adjudicating them informally.
- Prefer specific operating agreements over promises to “communicate better.”
- Include governance fixes when conflict is created by ambiguous roles or incentives.

## Output

Provide:

1. Suitability and escalation screen
2. Neutral conflict map
3. Interests, disputed facts, and power considerations
4. Session structure and mediator prompts
5. Option and agreement criteria
6. Written working agreement
7. Follow-up and breach/escalation process

## Quality check

- The plan does not imply blame before evidence.
- Formal-risk issues are routed appropriately.
- Agreements define observable behavior, decisions, and timelines.
- Power and retaliation risks are addressed.
- Root causes in roles, incentives, or process are not personalized away.

## Failure recovery

If one party refuses good-faith participation, move from mediation to leadership decision, policy enforcement, or formal investigation as appropriate. If facts remain disputed, agree on interim operating rules while evidence is reviewed.

## Example

**Request:** “Mediate recurring conflict between Security and IT over privileged access approvals.”

Separate role, risk, service-level, and trust issues; produce a facilitated session and enforceable operating agreement.

## Evaluation

A strong result is procedurally fair, psychologically safe, concrete, and durable under future disagreement.
