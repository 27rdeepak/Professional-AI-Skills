# SaaS Portfolio Governance

## Operating standard

Optimize for business capability, risk, and total lifecycle cost—not license count alone. Preserve differences between contracted seats, assigned seats, active usage, business-critical use, and technically removable access.

## Inputs

Obtain the application inventory, owners, capabilities, user populations, contracts, spend and renewal dates, usage data, data classifications, integrations, SSO and SCIM status, privileged roles, incidents, support burden, and exit constraints.

## Workflow

1. Reconcile inventory records across procurement, finance, identity, security, and business owners.
2. Map each application to business capabilities, critical processes, and accountable ownership.
3. Normalize cost into recurring, implementation, support, integration, migration, and exit components.
4. Assess adoption, duplication, service quality, security posture, compliance exposure, and lock-in.
5. Identify unused licenses, redundant tools, unmanaged applications, fragile integrations, and renewal cliffs.
6. Classify applications as invest, retain, optimize, consolidate, replace, retire, or investigate.
7. Quantify savings with implementation cost, timing, contract constraints, and downside.
8. Define governance gates for intake, security review, access, renewal, change, and retirement.

## Decision rules

- Do not retire a low-usage application until critical seasonal or specialist use is checked.
- Treat a negotiated discount as savings only against a credible baseline.
- Include migration, productivity disruption, data extraction, and dual-running cost.
- Escalate applications with sensitive data, unclear ownership, or no feasible exit path.
- Prefer capability consolidation only when workflow and integration costs support it.

## Output

Provide:

1. Portfolio baseline and data-quality caveats
2. Capability and duplication map
3. Cost, usage, risk, and renewal findings
4. Application disposition matrix
5. Savings and investment scenarios
6. 90-day actions and longer roadmap
7. Governance model, metrics, and decision rights

## Quality check

- Savings show formula, period, baseline, and one-time costs.
- Every disposition has an owner and dependency.
- Business disruption and exit risk are explicit.
- Security and identity controls influence, but do not solely determine, the decision.
- Unverified inventory gaps remain visible.

## Failure recovery

If inventory or usage is unreliable, create a reconciliation plan and provisional decisions only. Separate confirmed savings, potential savings, and avoided future cost.

## Example

**Request:** “Rationalize our 80 business applications before annual renewals.”

Return a disposition and renewal plan grounded in business capability, usage, total cost, risk, and implementation feasibility.

## Evaluation

A strong result is financially honest, operationally feasible, risk-aware, and tied to accountable decisions.
